# Construction Glossary (German-English)

## Purpose

> **Scope:** German-English terminology for construction, civil engineering, and
> concrete-durability subjects
> **Key items:** domain signals, terminology table, context forms, calque traps,
> untranslated terms

Load this glossary for translate tasks on construction and building-materials subjects.

Apply it for German to English translate tasks, per `process/translate-document.md`.

## Domain Signals

Apply this glossary when:

- The request names a construction, civil-engineering, or building-materials subject.
- The document covers exposure classes, corrosion processes, reinforcement, durability,
  or concrete structures.
- The document's terminology is dominated by material-degradation vocabulary -
  Karbonatisierung, Chloride, Frost-Tau-Wechsel, Sulfatangriff, Abrieb.

## Context Forms

Some German terms render differently by context - do not force one form everywhere:

- `Angriff` renders `attack` in process names (`Chloridangriff` - `chloride attack`),
  while `Einwirkung` in normative exposure descriptions renders `exposure` or `action` -
  `chloridhaltige Einwirkung` becomes `chloride exposure`.
- `Bewehrung` renders `reinforcement` - `rebar` is acceptable when the source means
  individual bars (`Bewehrungsstäbe`).
- `Betondeckung` renders `concrete cover` - never `concrete coverage` or `cover layer`.
- `Verschleiß` renders `wear` for the process - `Abrieb` renders `abrasion`, do not
  conflate them.
- `Rissbildung` renders `cracking` in most prose - `crack formation` for passages about
  the mechanism itself.
- `Gefügezerstörung` renders `structural degradation` - `microstructure destruction`
  for strictly material-science contexts.
- `unversehrt` and `intakt` both render `intact`.
- `Bauteil` renders `structural element` or `component` - `building part` is a calque.
- `freiliegende Bewehrung` renders `exposed reinforcement` - `freigelegte Bewehrung` is
  `reinforcement exposed by removal of cover`.

## Untranslated

Settled terms stay in their canonical form in English output:

- Exposure-class codes `X0`, `XC`, `XD`, `XS`, `XF`, `XA`, `XM` and their subdivisions
  (`XC1`-`XC4`, `XD1`-`XD3`, `XS1`-`XS3`, `XF1`-`XF4`, `XA1`-`XA3`, `XM1`-`XM3`) stay
  verbatim.
- Chemical formulas and ions `CO₂`, `Ca(OH)₂`, `CaCO₃`, `H₂O`, `NaCl`, `MgCl₂`,
  `SO₄²⁻`, `H⁺`, `Cl⁻` stay verbatim.
- Strength classes `C20/25`, `C35/45` and the ratio shorthand `w/z` stay verbatim -
  gloss `w/z` as `w/c` (water/cement ratio) on first use when the audience needs it.
- `pH`, `√t`, and the formula `x_c(t) ∝ √t` stay verbatim.
- Standard names `DIN EN 206`, `DIN 1045-2`, `DIN EN 1992-1-1` (`Eurocode 2`),
  `DIN 4030-1`, `DIN 1100`, `BAW-Merkblatt`, and `DAfStb-Heft` stay verbatim - the
  English reader keeps the standard's own designation.
